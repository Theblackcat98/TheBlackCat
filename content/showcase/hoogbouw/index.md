## What it is

The anonymous Dutch street building — the one that makes up whole city blocks and is almost never drawn — as a cutaway axonometric. Six floors at 5.3 units each, on a 3.5-unit structural bay.

## What was drawn

- Canted bay windows, the glazed corner bays that make these buildings read as Dutch rather than generic.
- Cornice and band courses at every floor line, with the piers between windows kept thin enough that the facade looks brick-built rather than panel-built.
- A stepped or Dutch gable crown at the top, with the pitched roof behind it and chimneys standing on the party walls.
- One bay opened up: the floor slabs, the party-wall section and the roof structure are exposed so the building reads as a stack of floors rather than a solid extrusion.
- A trap section through the roof to show how the pitched structure meets the gable.

## How it works

One self-contained HTML file: flat vertex-coloured surfaces, one light direction, ink linework in six weights over a paper-grain sheet. Comes with its own drafting context — dashed site grid, sketched shrubs, ghosted neighbours — so the building sits in a street rather than in space. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset.
