# Cross-Testament Typology Skill

Trace and evaluate authentic biblical typology, prophetic fulfillment trajectories, and covenantal continuity between the Old and New Testaments.

## Triggers
- User asks about types, shadows, and fulfillments in Scripture (e.g., "How is Adam a type of Christ in Romans 5?", "Explain Jesus as the Melchizedekian priest in Hebrews 7", "Connect the Passover lamb to the cross in John 19").
- User investigates intertextual echoes, Old Testament citations in the New Testament, or covenantal progression.
- User seeks to distinguish legitimate biblical typology from speculative or arbitrary allegorization.

## Typology vs. Allegory Distinction

To maintain exegetical integrity, distinguish between:
- **Biblical Typology**: Grounded in real, historical persons, events, or institutions ordained by God as preliminary models (*typos*) that find escalated fulfillment in Christ and the New Covenant (*antitypos*).
- **Unwarranted Allegory**: Reading hidden, symbolic, or spiritual meanings into historical details disconnected from the original author's intention and redemptive-historical progression.

```
[OT Type (Shadow/Pattern)] ──(Divinely Ordained Escalation)──> [NT Antitype (Christ/Fulfillment)]
• Historical Person/Event                                    • Cosmic/Spiritual Reality
• Real Earthly Anchor                                        • Greater & Final ("How Much More")
```

## The 6-Step Typological Analysis Protocol

### Step 1: Identify the OT Type (*Typos*)
Classify the historical Old Testament anchor into one of three categories:
1. **Persons**: Adam (Rom 5:14), Melchizedek (Gen 14 / Heb 7), Moses (Deut 18:15 / Acts 3:22), David (Ezek 34:23), Jonah (Matt 12:40).
2. **Institutions / Rites**: The Tabernacle/Temple (Exod 25 / John 1:14; 2:19), The Priesthood & Sacrificial System (Lev 16 / Heb 9), The Passover Lamb (Exod 12 / 1 Cor 5:7; John 19:36), The Feast of Firstfruits (Lev 23 / 1 Cor 15:20), The Day of Atonement (*Yom Kippur*).
3. **Events / Experiences**: The Exodus deliverance (Exod 14 / Luke 9:31 *exodos*), The Wilderness Manna & Water from Rock (Exod 16–17 / John 6; 1 Cor 10:4), The Bronze Serpent lifted up (Num 21 / John 3:14).

### Step 2: Establish the Historical Reality of the Type
Ensure the Old Testament passage is understood in its original grammatical-historical setting before drawing theological connections. The type is not a myth or cipher; its original earthly function must be clearly articulated.

### Step 3: Trace the Trajectory of Divine Intention
Examine intermediate scriptural developments across biblical history:
- Did the Old Testament prophets expand on this pattern (e.g., Davidic covenant in 2 Sam 7 expanded in Isaiah 9, 11 and Jeremiah 23; New Exodus motifs in Isaiah 40–55)?
- Did Second Temple literature or Psalms reflect upon this type (e.g., Psalm 110:4 applying Melchizedek to the Davidic Messiah)?

### Step 4: Analyze the New Testament Antitypical Fulfillment (*Antitypos*)
Demonstrate how the New Testament author identifies and unpacks the fulfillment in Christ or the New Covenant community.
- **Rule of Escalation (*A Fortiori* / "How Much More")**: The antitype is never a mere 1:1 copy; it is infinitely greater, higher, and final.
  - *Example*: Animal blood temporarily covered ritual impurity; Christ's blood eternally purges the conscience and redeems (Heb 9:13–14).

### Step 5: Evaluate Against Hays's 7 Intertextual Criteria
Test the validity of the proposed typological connection using Richard Hays's standard criteria:
1. **Availability**: Was the source text available to the author and audience?
2. **Volume**: How explicit and prominent are the verbal/conceptual markers in the text?
3. **Recurrence**: Is this pattern repeated elsewhere in the author's work or broader canon?
4. **Thematic Coherence**: Does the connection fit smoothly into the overarching argument of the text?
5. **Historical Plausibility**: Could the first-century author/readers have plausibly intended and understood this connection?
6. **History of Interpretation**: Has this connection been recognized by historical interpreters and the church?
7. **Illumination (Satisfaction)**: Does the connection clarify and enrich the meaning of the passage?

### Step 6: Formulate Practical & Doxological Takeaways
Conclude with the devotional and theological significance: How does recognizing this covenantal unity strengthen confidence in God's unified redemptive plan?

## Concrete Corpus Examples

### 1. Adam–Christ Typology in Romans 5:12–21 & 1 Corinthians 15:20–49
- **Type**: Adam — The representative head of the old creation. One act of disobedience brought sin, condemnation, and universal physical/spiritual death to all in him.
- **Antitype**: Christ — The representative head of the new creation (the "Second Adam" / "Last Adam"). One act of righteous obedience on the cross brings justification, righteousness, and eternal life to all in Him.
- **Escalation**: "For if many died through one man's trespass, **much more** (*pollō mallon*) have the grace of God and the free gift by the grace of that one man Jesus Christ abounded for many" (Rom 5:15).

### 2. The Passover Lamb in Exodus 12 & John 19:36 / 1 Corinthians 5:7
- **Type**: A spotless male lamb slain at twilight on 14 Nisan; blood applied to doorposts to avert the destroying angel; not a bone of the lamb broken (Exod 12:46; Num 9:12).
- **Antitype**: Jesus, the "Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (John 1:29), condemned at the sixth hour on Preparation Day (John 19:14), dying as the Passover sacrifice without having His legs broken (John 19:33, 36).
- **Escalation**: The Exodus lamb saved Israel's firstborn from temporal physical judgment in Egypt; Christ's sacrifice redeems people from every tribe and nation from eternal spiritual wrath and condemnation.

### 3. Melchizedekian Priest-King in Genesis 14:18–20, Psalm 110:4, and Hebrews 7
- **Type**: Melchizedek, King of Salem ("King of Peace") and Priest of God Most High (*El Elyon*), who meets Abraham without recorded genealogy, beginning of days, or end of life in the narrative.
- **Antitype**: Jesus Christ, King of Righteousness and King of Peace, whose priesthood is not based on Levitical genealogy or mortal succession, but on the "power of an indestructible life" (*kata dynamin zōēs akatalytou*, Heb 7:16).
- **Escalation**: The Levitical priests were mortal and required continuous animal offerings for their own sins; Christ is the sinless, eternal High Priest seated at the right hand of God, interceding forever.
