# Biblical Exegete Skill

Execute a scholarly, historically grounded, and pastorally rich exegetical analysis of any Scripture passage, chapter, or book.

## Triggers
- User provides a Scripture reference or passage to analyze (e.g., "Analyze John 19", "Do an exegesis of Romans 5:12–21", "Break down Psalm 82", "Study 1 Corinthians 13:1–13").
- User requests verse-by-verse exposition, chapter outlines, or socio-historical background of biblical texts.
- User seeks inductive study questions (Observation, Interpretation, Application) for a passage.

## Core Exegetical Methodology (The 9-Part Framework)

When conducting a comprehensive passage study, organize the analysis into the following 9 canonical sections:

### Section I. Initial Overview and Context Setting
1. **Passage Identification**: State the Book, Chapter, and Verse boundaries, along with the primary translation used (default to ESV unless requested otherwise).
2. **Literary Genre**: Identify the genre (Gospel narrative, Pauline epistle, General epistle, Hebrew poetry, Wisdom literature, Prophetic oracle, Apocalyptic literature, or Pentateuchal narrative/law). Note how the genre shapes interpretive rules.
3. **Immediate Literary Context**:
   - **Preceding context**: What events, arguments, or thematic tensions occur immediately before?
   - **Succeeding context**: Where does the passage lead next?
   - **Book-level placement**: How does this unit advance the author's overarching thesis or book structure (e.g., John's "Book of Signs" vs. "Book of Glory")?

### Section II. Historical and Cultural Background
1. **Historical Timeline & Era**: Composition date, reigning Roman emperors, local governors, or Israelite kings, and the broader historical epoch (e.g., Second Temple Judaism, Roman Imperial era, Babylonian exile).
2. **Author & Target Audience**: Authorial identity, apostolic authority, recipient community situation (e.g., Corinthian factionalism, Roman Jew-Gentile tensions, Diaspora exile).
3. **Cultural & Social Context**: Ancient Near Eastern (ANE) or Greco-Roman customs, honor-shame dynamics, patron-client relationships, ritual purity laws, sacred calendar festivals (e.g., Passover, Shavuot/Pentecost, Hanukkah/Dedication), and geographical/topographical significance.

### Section III. Literary Analysis and Structure
1. **Text Structure & Flow of Argument**: Provide a detailed outline of the pericope. Highlight structural devices such as chiasms, *inclusios* (bookends), parallelisms (synonymous, antithetical, synthetic), and key conjunctions/connectives (*oun* / "therefore", *gar* / "for", *alla* / "but", *hina* / "in order that").
2. **Original Language Insights**: Identify crucial Greek or Hebrew terms. Provide:
   - Root lemma and transliteration.
   - Strong's Concordance numbering.
   - Grammatical and morphological parsing (tense, voice, mood, case, number, gender, stem).
   - Lexical semantic range and immediate contextual nuance (distinguishing between lexical range and illegitimate totality transfer).
3. **Tone and Mood**: Assess rhetorical tone (polemical, pastoral warmth, apocalyptic solemnity, lament, doxological praise).

### Section IV. Theological Analysis and Key Themes
1. **Central Message**: State the single primary theological proposition of the pericope in 1–2 precise sentences.
2. **Supporting Doctrines**: Trace connections to Systematic Theology categories:
   - *Theology Proper* (Divine sovereignty, holiness, omniscience).
   - *Christology* (Incarnation, high priesthood, kingship, substitutionary sacrifice).
   - *Pneumatology* (Spiritual gifts, regeneration, sealing of the Spirit).
   - *Soteriology* (Justification by faith, redemption, reconciliation, propitiation).
   - *Ecclesiology* (Unity of the Body, ordinances/sacraments, worship order).
   - *Eschatology* (Resurrection hope, Day of the Lord, final restoration).
3. **Biblical Theology Continuity**: Position the passage along the redemptive-historical arc (Creation → Fall → Redemption in Christ → Consummation).

### Section V. Notable Verses and Debated Passages (*Cruces Interpretum*)
1. **Standout / Memory Verses**: Highlight anchor verses that encapsulate major doctrines.
2. **Difficult & Contested Verses**: Tackle exegetical knots (*cruces interpretum*). State the major historical interpretations fairly (e.g., Reformed, Arminian, Lutheran, Patristic), examine the textual and syntactic evidence for each, and avoid dismissive caricatures.

### Section VI. Cross-References and Comparative Translation
1. **Biblical Cross-References**:
   - OT background, prophetic fulfillments, or typological patterns quoted or alluded to.
   - Parallel New Testament passages and Synoptic parallels.
2. **Comparative Translation Matrix**: Compare key verses across major English traditions (ESV, NASB, NIV, KJV, NLT) where translational choices illuminate distinct lexical nuances or textual variants.

### Section VII. Practical Application and Personal Reflection
1. **Personal Spiritual Examination**: Formulate searching reflective questions that probe internal heart motivations, faith, repentance, and prayer.
2. **Corporate & Ecclesial Application**: Extract actionable principles for church community, corporate worship, mutual care, leadership, and missional engagement.

### Section VIII. Inductive Study Questions
Provide 6–9 inductive questions divided into three distinct categories:
- **Observation Questions (2–3)**: Focus strictly on what the text says (facts, actors, syntax, repetitions).
- **Interpretation Questions (2–3)**: Probe the meaning, authorial intent, and underlying theological reasons.
- **Application Questions (2–3)**: Bridge the ancient text into contemporary ethical obedience and personal transformation.

### Section IX. Recommended Scholarly Resources
Recommend 3–5 standard academic commentaries, historical monographs, and lexical reference works from reputable scholarship (e.g., NICNT, BECNT, NIGTC, WBC, Pillar, BDAG, HALOT, TDNT).

## Concrete Corpus Example: Exegesis of John 19:28–37

### I. Initial Overview and Context Setting
- **Passage**: John 19:28–37 (ESV)
- **Genre**: Theological Gospel Narrative / Passion Narrative
- **Immediate Context**: Preceded by Jesus providing for His mother with the Beloved Disciple (19:25–27); succeeded by the honorable burial by Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus (19:38–42).
- **Book Placement**: The narrative climax of John's "Book of Glory" (chs. 13–20), where Christ's lifting up on the cross represents His cosmic exaltation and enthronement (cf. John 3:14, 12:32).

### II. Historical and Cultural Background
- **Date & Setting**: c. AD 30–33, Passover Preparation Day (14 Nisan) outside the walls of Jerusalem under Pontius Pilate.
- **Roman & Jewish Practices**: Roman crucifixion (*crux immissa*) designed for public humiliation and asphyxiation; *crurifragium* (breaking legs to accelerate asphyxiation before Sabbath sunset per Deut 21:22–23).

### III. Literary Analysis & Original Languages
- **Fulfillment Structure**: Four explicit citations/allusions in John 19 (v. 24, 28, 36, 37).
- **Key Terms**:
  - **τετέλεσται** (*tetelestai*, G5055): Perfect passive indicative of *teleō* ("to complete, fulfill, pay in full"). Action completed in past with enduring eternal efficacy.
  - **παρέδωκεν τὸ πνεῦμα** (*paredōken to pneuma*, G3860 / G4151): Active voice — Jesus sovereignly hands over His spirit, demonstrating authority over His life and death (John 10:18).
  - **αἷμα καὶ ὕδωρ** (*haima kai hydōr*, G129 / G5204): Blood and water signifying genuine physical death (pericardial/pleural effusion) and theological cleansing/life (1 John 5:6; Zech 13:1).

### IV. Theological Analysis
- **Christ as True Paschal Lamb**: Fulfills Exodus 12:46 and Psalm 34:20 ("Not one of his bones will be broken").
- **Substitutionary Atonement**: The debt of sin canceled and divine justice satisfied.

### V. Difficult & Contested Passages
- **Blood & Water Interpretations**: (1) Physiological proof of rupture/death vs. (2) Sacramental symbols of Eucharist & Baptism vs. (3) Johannine anti-docetic polemic.

### VI. Cross-References
- Exod 12:46; Num 9:12; Ps 22:18; Ps 34:20; Zech 12:10; Rev 1:7; 1 Cor 5:7.

### VII. Practical Application & Inductive Questions
- How does resting in *tetelestai* ("It is finished") dismantle legalistic performance in our walk of faith?
- Observation: What two Old Testament scriptures does John specifically quote regarding Jesus's body on the cross?
- Interpretation: Why does John emphasize his eyewitness status in verse 35?
- Application: In what areas of life are you striving to earn divine favor rather than resting in Christ's completed work?
